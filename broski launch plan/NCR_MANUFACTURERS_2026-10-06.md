## Broski call and RFQ: same brief for every candidate

**Opening:** “We are a West Delhi startup evaluating one existing, documented dog reward-biscuit recipe under Broski. Please confirm whether you manufacture biscuits yourself and can deliver 500 accepted sealed 200 g retail packs to [West Delhi PIN]. This is an enquiry, not an order.”

1. **Factory identity:** Who owns the oven/line? Exact food-plant address, legal selling entity, invoice/GST details and bank beneficiary? Can we visit by appointment or obtain a live site walkthrough and manufacturing documents? If a trader, identify actual manufacturer rather than concealing it.
2. **Actual product fit:** Existing baked recipe, intended use/age range, formulation revision, ingredient/allergen list, specialist oversight? What changes to flavour, size or pack invalidate existing validation? Do not commission a new recipe merely to match a website mock-up.
3. **Quantity:** Minimum by recipe and packaging artwork? Quote 500 accepted packs, not 100 kg of bulk. Nominal retail fill is 100 kg; yield loss, overfill, lab samples and retained stock require an agreed additional allowance. If smaller documented pilot is possible, quote it separately.
4. **QA evidence:** Request traceability, ingredient controls, documented process, lot COA and relevant lab reports. Qualified pet-food technologist/veterinary nutrition reviewer and laboratory must set appropriate safety/chemical/microbiological criteria. No recipe or universal numerical test limits are supplied here.
5. **Shelf life:** Evidence for the exact recipe and final packaging/storage conditions? Ask which existing data transfers, what additional work is needed and who signs release. A competitor's best-before date or generic “12 months” promise is insufficient.
6. **Packaging:** Stock opaque/metallized barrier pouch with zipper plus top heat seal, actual 200 g fill test, two labels and coding at the food plant. Ask layer structure, contact/migration evidence, moisture/oxygen barrier values with test conditions, sealing settings and integrity checks. A pouch marketed as “200 g” may not fit bulky biscuits.
7. **Price:** Itemize product, pouch, labels, packing/sealing/coding, master cartons, inward delivery, tax, one-time development/testing and wastage. Who owns spare packaging? Quote validity and accepted-pack costing. No home repacking assumption.
8. **Terms:** Samples first; agreed specification and final artwork; deposit only after identity and evidence review; production milestones, inspection/hold, rejected-batch remedy, replacement freight, recall cooperation, formulation/IP rights and no substitutions without approval. Contract/legal review remains required.

**Copyable email/message:**

Subject: Broski West Delhi pilot — biscuit manufacturing / 500 × 200 g accepted retail packs

We are evaluating one existing documented reward-biscuit SKU, sealed and labelled at your plant, delivered to [PIN]. Please distinguish biscuit capability from adjacent treats. Our planning range is ₹75–95 per accepted landed pack, subject to safe compliant scope; it is not an agreed price. If unavailable, quote your smallest suitable pilot separately. No manufacture or artwork printing is authorized by this enquiry.

## Budget and acceptance decision

The total business ceiling is ₹2,00,000, not the manufacturer spending allowance. Prior plan's 500 × ₹85 = ₹42,500 inventory cost is unquoted; ₹75–95 yields ₹37,500–47,500. Keep QA, consultant, label/legal review, samples, outbound shipping, acquisition and contingency separately visible. Recalculate with actual tax treatment and customer-order carton costs; never compare an untested bulk ₹/kg listing directly against finished branded retail-pack cost.

Founder can spend the first week on four screening calls (Pet Zone, Innomalous, TrueCare, Mannu-G) and two written follow-ups; substitute Biovencer if biscuit capability is rejected. Obtain comparable written offers before physical samples. No deposit based on this document alone. Hold or reject a candidate for missing identity/traceability, unsupported safety/stability, unexplained beneficiary mismatch or refusal of the agreed manufacturing specification. A high MOQ is simply a poor commercial fit. If no nearby supplier clears the gates within budget, obtain the national OEM comparison or reduce scope/delay; do not force a nearby unverified producer to satisfy an arbitrary launch date.
