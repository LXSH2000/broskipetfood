# Broski — manufacturing and packaging sourcing plan

Research checked 30 September 2026. India launch; total business budget below ₹2,00,000. This document is a sourcing shortlist and negotiation brief, not supplier endorsement, a quotation, formulation advice or legal clearance. No supplier has been contacted and no purchase has been placed. Business-strategist workflow used: validate assumptions before committing inventory.

## Recommended production route

Launch one 200 g biscuit SKU using an established domestic manufacturer's already validated formulation, packed and sealed at its facility in stock barrier pouches with Broski labels. Prefer an existing recipe/packaging combination supported by applicable stability evidence; commissioning a new recipe and pretending its shelf life is established in a few weeks is not a viable shortcut.

Request 500 accepted finished packs, not simply 100 kg of bulk product. 500 × 200 g = 100 kg nominal retail fill; process loss, check samples, retained packs and any permitted overfill require additional output. Ask the manufacturer to price those explicitly. Do not assume that 100 kg purchased guarantees 500 saleable packs.

**Planning assumption:** ₹75–95 per accepted 200 g pack landed at Broski's storage location, or ₹37,500–47,500 for 500 packs. This target includes product, primary pouch, labels, filling, sealing, batch coding, master cartons, inward freight and non-recoverable taxes. It excludes one-time sample development, independent testing/consultation, launch creative, individual customer shipping and marketing. GST treatment and recoverable credits must be confirmed before comparing quotations. A quote above the target requires a revised margin model or smaller validated pilot, never reduced safety checks.

## Packaging: practical low-quantity launch

Use a stock opaque or metallized heat-sealable stand-up pouch with zipper plus two digitally printed labels; the zipper is for reclosure, the top heat seal provides the unopened seal. Buy samples before selecting size. A supplier's “200 g” capacity usually refers to an assumed product density: bulky dog biscuits may require a larger pouch. Test 200 g of the actual biscuit, seal clearance, breakage and carton packing.

**Verified benchmark:** [Restokart 5 × 8.25 inch silver stand-up zipper pouch](https://www.restokart.com/product/5x825-inches-silver-standup-zipper-pouch/660) lists ₹3.74/piece and packs of 100; 500 is ₹1,870 before any tax/freight not shown. The page specifies PET/AL/PE on the silver side and PET/PE on the transparent side. This is a low-MOQ cost benchmark, not approval of moisture/oxygen protection for Broski. Request food-contact declaration, migration information, thickness, OTR/WVTR and heat-seal parameters; the clear face may be insufficient for the chosen recipe/shelf life. Do not claim recyclable based on appearance; the vendor notes standard-stream recycling limitations.

**Labels:** The search-indexed supplier listing for [HelloPrints product-label catalogue](https://www.helloprints.in/product?id=product-labels) advertises a minimum of 100 labels and paper/vinyl material options. Direct page retrieval was unavailable; reconfirm these details before relying on them. RFQ exact size, two designs, adhesive, rub/oil resistance, final tax/freight, sample proof and delivery; no final price is assumed here. Obtain a local digital-print quote as a comparison. Planning allowance, not quote: ₹4–8 combined front/back labels per pack; 500 packs require 1,000 labels plus agreed spoilage allowance. Label sizes must leave the heat-seal area and batch-coding window clear.

Budget illustration inside the landed target: pouch ₹5–8, paired labels ₹4–8, product/factory packing ₹55–65, inward freight/master-carton/tax contingency ₹11–14 = ₹75–95. Every component except the specific Restokart benchmark is an assumption. If factory packing already includes pouches or cartons, remove duplicates. Avoid printed-film cylinders and thousands of custom pouches before repeat demand is demonstrated.

Order about 550 stock pouches and 550 label pairs only after proof and fit approval; the exact quantity should follow the factory's agreed wastage/sample plan. Keep unused packaging available for pilot corrections. Let the contract manufacturer pack; do not open bulk product and repack at home without an appropriately assessed operation.

## QA and evidence gate before commercial release

Engage a qualified pet-food technologist and veterinarian with relevant nutrition expertise to review the manufacturer's formulation, intended use, ingredient/allergen controls and feeding statements. This plan provides no recipe. A dog biscuit is not automatically a complete diet; do not print complete-and-balanced, therapeutic, dental, hypoallergenic or other functional claims without substantiation and appropriate review.

[TÜV SÜD India](https://www.tuvsud.com/en-in/industries/consumer-products-and-retail/food/shelf-life-testing) explicitly includes animal feed/pet food in its shelf-life experience and discusses microbial, moisture/water-activity, rancidity, sensory and packaging-integrity testing. Request a written pet-treat-specific protocol and quotation. Confirm the actual laboratory's accreditation scope for each commissioned method, rather than relying on a logo. Ask the specialist to define microbiological, chemical and contaminant limits; do not invent universal thresholds.

Obtain: signed ingredient/formulation revision; ingredient supplier traceability; allergen controls; production lot traceability; validated process evidence; lot certificate of analysis; independent initial batch testing appropriate to the ingredients; moisture/water-activity and oxidation assessment where relevant; final-pack stability evidence; storage conditions; retained-sample schedule; and complaint/recall contacts. Testing may include pathogens, yeast/mould, indicator organisms, ingredient-dependent mycotoxins/heavy metals, nutritional analysis for label declarations and packaging migration, as specified by the qualified laboratory/technologist.

Use the final recipe and final pouch for stability work. Existing manufacturer data only transfers where the expert confirms comparability. Accelerated testing is not automatic proof of any chosen best-before period. Do not promise a 30-day commercial launch if sufficient safety/stability evidence is unavailable. Verify applicable current standards using [BIS Know Your Standard](https://www.bis.gov.in/know-your-standard/?lang=en); BIS's published programme references IS 11968:2019 for dog/cat pet food, reviewed 2024. Determine applicability to the actual complementary treat with a competent adviser; do not claim BIS certification or that a supplier's FSSAI marketing statement authorizes Broski.

## Packaged-batch acceptance/rejection schedule

Attach a signed specification and a lab-approved sampling plan to the purchase order. Suggested commercial controls below are proposals, not statutory sampling tolerances:

| Check | Acceptance evidence | Hold/reject action |
|---|---|---|
| Identity and traceability | Approved recipe, supplier, lot code, packaging artwork and dates match documents | Quarantine any mismatch; no substitute formulation without written approval |
| Microbiological/chemical safety | Reports meet expert-approved limits/methods for this recipe and use | Entire lot held pending investigation on any safety failure; no dilution/relabel shortcut |
| Weight | Calibrated scale records and net-content checks under applicable legal requirements | Hold affected lot; factory rework/replacement and repeat verification |
| Seal and pouch | Continuous seal; no leak, puncture, contamination or damaged zipper; agreed seal tests pass | Any critical seal/contamination defect triggers hold and investigation; segregate affected production |
| Product condition | No mould, pests, foreign bodies or rancid/off odour; agreed reference colour/texture | Critical defect: reject/hold lot, trace cause; visual check does not replace lab release |
| Breakage | Propose at most 5% crumbs/broken pieces by weight in agreed transit-test sample; negotiate definition | Excess: factory corrective action/credit/replacement; never resell unsafe material |
| Label legibility | Correct SKU, quantity, responsible entities, consumer contact, batch/date/MRP information; no unapproved claims | Hold for lawful relabelling before sale; preserve traceability |
| Shelf-life evidence | Expert-approved supported period and storage statement; propose at least 85% remaining at receipt | Reject short-dated stock unless separately assessed and accepted in writing |

Factory checks every pack for coding/seal appearance; independent lot inspection follows agreed sampling. Record photos, sealed retained samples and release signoff. FEFO storage: first expiry, first out; keep dry, pest controlled, off floor and away from heat/sunlight. Damaged returned packs are quarantined and never returned to saleable stock.

## Manufacturing agreement: clauses to negotiate

Use a written agreement reviewed for the actual parties/product. Include legal entity/GST details and plant address; approved formula and artwork annexes; no ingredient/process/subcontractor substitution without permission; MOQ and accepted finished-pack count; itemized price, GST and freight; samples and testing responsibilities; shelf-life basis; traceability and audit access; production dates; proof-approval process; change control; ownership/licence of formula versus Broski brand/artwork; confidentiality; non-use of your branded packaging for others; agreed wastage; remaining-packaging ownership; staged payment; inspection/rejection/rework terms; delayed/failed-batch remedies; product liability/insurance allocation; recall notification and cost responsibility; retention of batch records; termination; and dispute venue.

Negotiate deposit after sample/spec approval, balance against production documents and agreed release inspection where commercially possible. Do not describe proposed terms as accepted supplier policy. Verify bank beneficiary against the contracted legal entity before paying.

## Label plan — draft checklist for qualified review

Front: Broski; “Treats for your best bro”; accurate common product name and flavour; “Dog treats”/intended complementary use where appropriate; net quantity 200 g. Keep attractive generated artwork separate from legally approved print files.

Back: approved ingredient list and allergen/cross-contact disclosure; analytical/nutritional declarations as required and supported; suitable dog age/use and expert-approved feeding guidance; storage/reseal guidance; manufacturer/packer and marketer names/addresses; consumer care phone/email; batch/lot; manufacture/packing dates and supported best-before/use-by information; MRP inclusive of taxes; other applicable Legal Metrology declarations and registration/licence details only where legally applicable. Check ecommerce declarations against physical pack. A barcode is commercial infrastructure, not a safety certificate. Do not print unverified logos, certification numbers, “vet approved,” disease claims, artificial stars/testimonials or a shelf-life guessed from competitors. Final legal applicability/signoff is a launch dependency, not completed by this checklist.

## Sequencing and budget discipline

Days 1–7: founder requests three quotes, checks identity and compares scope. Days 8–21: paid samples, fit/transit checks, specialist document review, lab protocol and artwork proof. Following approval: allow roughly 3–6 weeks for a small existing-recipe production run as a planning estimate, plus any unmet stability/testing time. Actual lead times and 500-pack acceptance remain unconfirmed. New formulation may take substantially longer.

Obtain quotes for one-time samples/consultation/lab work before deposit; those costs sit outside the ₹37,500–47,500 inventory allowance. Keep the aggregate launch budget below ₹2 lakh. If evidence costs or MOQ do not fit, reduce inventory/scope or delay release. Do not spend the safety reserve on custom packaging or a second SKU.
