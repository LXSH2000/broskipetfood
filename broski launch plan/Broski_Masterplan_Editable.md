## 08 / Safe product release

BROSKI / QA AND SHELF LIFE

08 / Safe product release

Do not formulate by copying internet recipes. Use a qualified pet-food technologist and a veterinarian with relevant nutrition expertise to assess the manufacturer’s formulation and intended use. Budget ₹22,000 for the agreed review/testing scope; secure quotes before assuming that scope fits.

Gate | Required evidence

Recipe and process | Signed formulation revision, ingredient traceability, allergen controls, relevant heat/process validation and change-control records.

Testing protocol | Qualified lab specifies microbiological, chemical, moisture/water-activity and ingredient-dependent contaminants; methods and acceptance limits agreed in writing.

Shelf-life evidence | Exact recipe + exact final pouch + storage conditions. Existing validated evidence can support review; a new formulation may need longer studies.

Factory batch | Lot COA, production date, approved coding, count/weight checks, sealed packs and manufacturing records.

Receipt and release | No pests, mould, rancid smell, wrong labels, broken seals or untraceable lots. Quarantine failures; retain agreed samples.

TÜV SÜD India describes pet-food/animal-feed shelf-life work [S9]; it is a laboratory lead, not a booked service or price quote. Verify the actual lab’s accreditation scope for the commissioned methods. Specialists define acceptance thresholds; this plan does not invent universal limits.

Complaints and withdrawal

For suspected contamination, illness, foreign matter or a serious seal failure: stop selling the affected lot, preserve evidence, contact the manufacturer/specialist, trace buyers and record advice/actions. Withdraw or recall as directed. Do not diagnose an animal or dismiss a safety complaint as a refund issue.

Decision gate: Do not print a 6- or 12-month shelf life by borrowing a competitor date. Failed evidence moves the launch date; it does not reduce the test scope.

## 10 / Label and compliance checklist

BROSKI / CONFIRM BEFORE PRINTING

10 / Label and compliance checklist

The Food Safety and Standards Act defines food for human consumption and excludes animal feed [S10]. A human-food FSSAI licence is therefore not a default answer for dog treats. Confirm the actual pet-treat business, plant and local requirements with competent professionals/authorities.

Workstream | Action before launch

State/local operation | Identify city, storage/packing roles and supplier premises; confirm applicable trade, establishment, manufacturing/animal-feed and other permissions.

BIS | IS 11968:2019 is a pet-food standard [S11]. Confirm scope for complementary biscuits and whether any applicable mandatory order/certification exists; do not self-claim certified status.

Legal Metrology | Reviewer confirms manufacturer/packer responsibility and registration, net quantity, MRP inclusive of taxes, identity/address, consumer care, dates and applicable unit-price/ecommerce declarations [S12].

GST | CA confirms current HSN, rate, registration, interstate/ecommerce position, input credit and shipping invoicing. This model’s 18% remains provisional: the historical CBIC FAQ was found, but current amended notifications were not fully reconciled [S18–S19].

Plastic packaging | Map Brand Owner vs Producer/Importer status. CPCB FAQ discusses micro/small Brand Owner EPR relief, not a universal exemption for every MSME role [S13].

Brand/IP | Search and clear name before artwork. Eligible trademark e-filing government fee published at ₹4,500 per class/application [S14]; professional/search fees extra. Udyam registration is free [S3].

Back label: approved ingredients/allergens; substantiated analytical/use/feeding/storage information; manufacturer/packer and marketer details; consumer care; traceable lot; manufacture/packing and justified best-before information; approved MRP and applicable declarations. Front: accurate name, dog-treat use, net 200 g, brand. A final legal label review is required.

Decision gate: Do not print therapeutic, complete-and-balanced, hypoallergenic, dental or “vet approved” claims, certification marks or generated endorsements without substantiation and permission.

Follow up only through the supplier's published business channels and retain the quotation. Do not send an advance to an unrelated individual.

Call result: proceed to samples; ask for missing documents; category mismatch; MOQ unaffordable; or decline. Keep a backup lead active until the primary sample/specification and written quote pass. An attractive brochure does not override failed quality evidence.

4. Packing choices that matter

Primary pouch protects the product; front/back label communicates the approved facts; shipping carton protects the parcel in transit. Keep these costs separate in quotes. The base ₹85 landed pack already includes primary pouch, labels, sealing/coding and inbound delivery assumptions. The ₹18 per order shipping material is additional.

Recommended provisional route: opaque or metallized stand-up zipper pouch, heat sealed above the zipper by the manufacturer, with professionally reviewed durable labels. Ask for OTR and WVTR, material structure, intended-contact suitability and seal parameters. “Food grade,” thickness, silver colour or a zipper alone do not prove shelf life.

Pack-fit test: fill the actual approved biscuit into two or three sample pouch sizes; verify usable headspace and seal area, whether biscuits crumble, label fit, print readability and the packed weight/dimensions. Give the final pouch specification to the qualified shelf-life reviewer. Do not extrapolate the tested shelf life from a different pouch.

For first run, skip clear windows, custom-shaped bags, print cylinders, rigid gift boxes and elaborate inserts unless supplier evidence and the same cash envelope justify them. A window needs its own barrier assessment; an expensive unboxing must earn back its cost. Laminated pouches may have recycling limitations, so no unsupported eco-friendly claims.

6. Customer questions before the batch

Ask about the last actual treat purchase: brand, amount paid, weight, channel, delivery time and how long it lasted. Ask why it was chosen and what the dog accepted or rejected. Ask whether ingredients, texture, size or resealing affected use. Avoid collecting unnecessary sensitive details.

Show the proposed actual prices and delivery charges without claiming a product is ready. Ask “Which would you buy, and what makes you hesitate?” rather than “Do you like our idea?” Ask what proof an unfamiliar brand needs and how the buyer would discover it. Record objections verbatim with consent, then group repeated themes.

Interview 20 buyers across weeks 1–3; short sessions plus notes must fit the founder's weekly acquisition time. Interested friends are a convenience sample, not representative national demand. Take no paid orders before the product and commerce gates pass.

7. A useful launch dashboard

Metric | Definition / action

Delivered orders | Orders actually received; distinguish pending, canceled and returned.

Net sales | Actual collections less applicable tax, refunds and adjustments.

Contribution | Net sales minus stock, freight, packing, payment, losses and acquisition.

New-customer CAC | Attributed acquisition expenditure / new delivered customers; small samples are noisy.

Blended acquisition spend | All acquisition spend / all delivered orders; different from paid-channel CAC.

Repeat cohort | Buyers with enough elapsed time to reorder; report count and denominator.

Quality incidents | Count by lot/severity; serious safety issue stops affected sales immediately.

Cash protection | Paid + committed spend, tax/refund liabilities, stock and remaining reserve.

Use one weekly sheet, one order/lot ledger and one complaint log. Decisions should follow delivered-order economics. Views, followers and influencer reach are supporting signals, not profit.

8. Stop, repair or grow

No viable supplier or evidence within the cap: pause the pilot and seek a smaller documented run. Do not buy equipment to escape an inconvenient MOQ. A resale fallback needs written rights, compliant handling and new economics; it is not permission to relabel somebody else's packs.

Positive contribution but slow demand: improve the repeated customer objection, narrow the audience and retest within the remaining acquisition cap. Negative contribution: change price, freight/service area, landed cost or channel before adding advertising. Quality uncertainty: stop release and resolve it with competent specialists.

Reorder only with actual delivered margins, resolved complaints, expiry/stock reconciliation, repeat-buy evidence and sufficient cash for the full next batch plus taxes/refunds while preserving the reserve. The pilot has no funded second production run in its revenue scenarios.

9. The brand's evidence ladder

Stage one: concept art and truthful waitlist. Stage two: actual approved samples, packaging proof and supplier/quality checks. Stage three: released stock and verified checkout. Stage four: real delivered feedback with permission. Stage five: measured repeat purchase and controlled expansion.

Match marketing to the stage actually achieved. Photos of generated packs may illustrate the concept; actual stock photos establish what the buyer will receive. Do not substitute professional-looking presentation for evidence.
