# Broski strategy council: execution brief

1 October 2026. Decision: validate one outsourced 200 g complementary reward-biscuit SKU in one reachable Indian city cluster. Hard total cash ceiling INR 200,000; acquisition ceiling INR 15,000; founder capacity 10 hours/week. This is a staged experiment, not a guaranteed high-growth business. Strategy skill applied: agency-business-strategist. No outreach or spending executed.

## Evidence and strategic interpretation

HUFT's official store markets goat-milk/blueberry cookies at a displayed INR 299/200 g; Dogsee markets carrot cookies at INR 199/300 g, with no stock displayed during research. Their ingredient-led positioning means “natural biscuits for dogs” is already crowded. These observations do not establish either firm's market share, service weaknesses or profitability. Sources accessed 30 September–1 October 2026: [HUFT product](https://headsupfortails.com/products/blueberries-goat-milk-dog-cookies), [Dogsee product](https://dogseechew.in/product/dogsee-gigabites-carrot-cookies-for-dogs). Store crawls can lag; recheck prices before using comparative advertising.

**Inference:** the practical opening for a tiny entrant is a tightly served local cohort, traceable batches, clear feeding/ingredient information and responsive replenishment. These are hypotheses and execution standards, not an exclusive moat. Established brands can copy them. Relationships, reliable operations and repeat-purchase evidence may create a small local advantage over time.

WhatsApp's official help describes a business catalog for product display; this supports a simple assisted-sale channel, not proof of demand or guaranteed native payments. Use catalog links and the tested storefront/payment provider; avoid API subscriptions at pilot scale. Obtain opt-in, honour stop requests, and never scrape or buy pet-owner numbers. [WhatsApp catalog](https://faq.whatsapp.com/405903568419894/?helpref=uf_share).

Local groomer/apartment-community referrals are proposed tests, not researched availability or endorsements. Get each host's permission. ASCI guidance treats free products and other material connections as requiring prominent influencer disclosure; pay for agreed content, not positive opinions. [ASCI influencer FAQs](https://www.ascionline.in/social/faqs/). Marketplace expansion is deferred until current category approval, fees, settlement and return costs are quoted. No invented marketplace margin or national market-size claim is needed to validate 100 local orders.

## Three concepts to test before choosing packaging

| Concept | Customer job / promise to test | Evidence test | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Everyday reward ritual — selected | Adult-dog owner wants an easy, enjoyable treat after a walk, with readable ingredients and straightforward reorder | At least 8 of 20 interviews report a recurring treat occasion; at least 10 of 20 qualified sample households later pay the stated price | Taste preference alone does not produce repeat purchase |
| B. Pocket training rewards | Owner wants small, clean-handling pieces for repeated reinforcement | Demonstrate verified piece dimensions, breakability, calories and crumbling with supplier-approved product; interview 5 trainers/owners | Current biscuit recipe may be unsuitable; “training” requires physical/product fit |
| C. Local dog-parent gift | Buyer wants a presentable small gift for another dog owner | Show a digital gift-card sleeve concept to 10 buyers; seek 3 price-specific expressions of intent before any extras | Seasonal demand, extra packaging cost, poor replenishment |

Use separate concept cards with identical product image, weight and price to reduce confounding. Expressions of intent are weaker than paid purchases. Select A initially; retain B/C as research alternatives, not extra stock keeping units. If interviews reject A, change the concept before manufacturing. No second flavour, therapeutic promise, subscription billing or gift-box inventory in the pilot.

**Proposed positioning:** “Broski: a little reward for your everyday dog-parent moments. One clear recipe, a traceable batch, and easy local reordering.” Only publish “one clear recipe” when ingredients and supplier controls exist. Final flavour, texture, suitable age/size and feeding quantities must follow validated formulation and professional review. Do not claim suitability for every dog or medical benefit.

## Working customer persona and job

Research hypothesis: a 25–45-year-old dog owner in a serviceable urban neighbourhood, living with an adult companion dog, already buying packaged treats at least monthly, and willing to inspect ingredients before trying a new brand. Household income is not assumed; observe actual spending. Early recruitment favours reachable people beyond friends/family who can afford the stated offer without inducement.

Functional job: reward the dog conveniently without confusing pack information. Emotional job: feel confident about the choice and enjoy a shared routine. Social job: share a useful local recommendation without risking friends' trust. Anxieties: dog refusal, unsuitable ingredients, stale/broken product, confusing delivery fees, unknown seller. Triggers to investigate: running out, a trusted groomer recommendation, seeing a truthful demonstration. Likely channels to test: permitted apartment pet groups, local groomers, opt-in WhatsApp and Instagram content; channel preference is not yet established.

## Twenty discovery questions

Ask about actual past behaviour before showing the product. Record anonymised notes; request consent for contact and any recording separately.

1. Tell me about the last treat you bought: brand, pack, price and shop.
2. When did you last give a treat, and what was happening?
3. How many days a week do you use treats?
4. How long did your last pack last?
5. Who chooses and who pays for the treats?
6. What alternatives do you use, including homemade food or no treat?
7. What caused you to try your current brand?
8. What made you stop buying a previous brand?
9. What does your dog regularly refuse?
10. What ingredients or textures do you avoid, and why?
11. Has a vet given feeding restrictions that affect your choices?
12. What pack information do you actually check before buying?
13. Tell me about the last late, damaged or stale delivery.
14. What would make you trust a new seller enough to try once?
15. Where do you usually discover pet products?
16. Have you acted on a groomer, creator or friend recommendation recently?
17. Would you prefer collecting locally or paying for delivery, and what did you choose last time?
18. Looking at INR 249 plus INR 59 delivery, INR 449 for two, and INR 649 for three, which would you choose today—or none—and why?
19. What would have to happen before you reordered this exact product?
20. May we invite you to an optional, clearly explained product evaluation after quality clearance, with no obligation to post or buy?

Do not recruit dogs with known restrictions into sampling without appropriate owner/veterinary clearance. Stop an evaluation on any reported adverse reaction and follow the quality escalation procedure; owner satisfaction cannot establish safety.

## Offer and economic guardrails

Keep the preliminary offers unchanged: one 200 g pack INR 249 + INR 59 delivery; two identical packs INR 449 delivered; three identical packs INR 649 delivered, only on confirmed economical routes. Show actual total prices prominently. Bundles are quantity choices, not additional SKUs. No crossed-out fabricated price or artificial scarcity.

Under the existing finance assumptions—INR 85 landed per pack, provisional 18% output GST, freight INR 65/75/85, outer pack INR 18, gateway 2.36%, loss reserve INR 15—contribution before acquisition is INR 70.75/91.91/161.68. These are conditional calculations, not supplier quotes. At INR 60 acquisition cost, contribution becomes INR 10.75/31.91/101.68. Prioritise the three-pack for cold advertising; offer the single honestly for trial. Revised finance calculations supersede these if supplier or tax facts change.

Track customer acquisition cost as all attributable channel cash, commissions and consumed gifted-product cost divided by first-time delivered, non-refunded customers. Also track campaign media-only CAC separately. Existing scenarios use blended acquisition spend/all orders; never label that true new-customer CAC. Discounts reduce revenue and cannot disappear into an off-book marketing line. Do not assume lifetime value until repeat cohorts mature.

Illustrative cold funnel, not a forecast: INR 3,000 media at INR 10/click produces 300 visits. At 2% delivered first-order conversion, six customers imply INR 500 media CAC: uneconomic. With INR 60 target media CAC, INR 10 CPC would require 16.7% conversion. Therefore paid advertising is a small feasibility test, not the sales engine. Real attribution should use source codes plus an optional checkout source question, excluding cancelled/refunded orders.

Stock for sampling is already inside the 500-pack purchase, not an extra purchase from this allocation. Proposed 50-pack reserve: 20 consenting consumer evaluations, 5 creator gifts, 5 partner evaluation packs, 10 QA/retained packs, 10 replacements. The quality professional decides actual retention quantity first; reduce marketing gifts if more retention is needed. Use sealed, approved packs; do not improvise unlabelled sample repacking. At INR 85/pack, all 50 cost INR 4,250 already counted in inventory; 30 outreach packs consume INR 2,550 of product value. Include consumed gifts in channel economics even though the cash was paid from inventory. Sample delivery/logistics must fit the specified marketing amounts, not silently expand freight budgets.

Creator selection: inspect local relevance, authentic audience comments and previous disclosures; follower count alone is insufficient. Brief requests an honest usage demonstration, actual pack/price, clear sponsored/gift disclosure and no medical or invented endorsement claims. Request permission before reusing content. Never label free-sample opinions verified purchases. Publish criticism fairly and handle safety complaints privately and promptly without suppressing reports.

Groomer model starts as referral-to-Broski, not wholesale: customer pays Broski, normal published offer applies, Broski fulfils, partner earns the capped referral fee. Do not also assume a retail margin. A later reseller route requires its own ex-tax margin, invoice, expiry/returns and credit model. No consignment stock or unpaid receivables in this pilot.

Timing is tested against audience response, not called universally optimal.

## Stop, refine and scale rules

These are management thresholds for a small pilot, not statistically proven benchmarks.

- Immediate hold: unresolved product safety/seal/traceability issue, misleading claim, failed compliance/quality gate or insufficient fulfilment cash. Escalate complaints through the quality owner.
- Refine before ads if fewer than 8/20 interviews reveal a recurring relevant job; avoid manufacturing merely because friends like the logo.
- Review sample conversion after all 20 evaluated households have 14 days to decide: target at least 10 paid first orders at the normal offer. If fewer than 5 buy, pause new acquisition and investigate price/product fit; do not count gifts as sales.
- Review each INR 1,500 ad tranche after a full seven-day attribution window and delivery reconciliation. Stop if no plausible positive contribution after actual acquisition cost; never spend the second tranche automatically. A few orders are directional only.
- Release the INR 3,000 winning-channel reserve only after at least 20 attributable first-time delivered orders, observed total CAC at or below INR 60, positive contribution after actual losses, and no unresolved quality issues. If the winning channel cannot be measured, retain the reserve.
- Measure 30-day repeat rate only among customers whose first delivered order is at least 30 days old. Require at least 20 eligible customers before interpreting the pilot target of 20% repeat. If the selling window is too short, report immature data and extend observation without a reorder commitment.
- Reorder only against verified demand, stock/expiry reconciliation, confirmed next-batch cost and a cash forecast protecting taxes, refunds and INR 30,000 contingency. Scale a proven channel in small increments; 90-day revenue growth or full startup-cost recovery is not promised.

## Council challenge questions

Finance: does INR 85 genuinely include tax, pouch, label and inbound? What happens at higher billable weight? Can INR 40 commission plus gifts preserve margin? Is CAC measured over new delivered customers? Is the 18% tax assumption confirmed? Supplier/QA: is one 500-pack run available; is the recipe appropriate to the claimed use; what evidence validates shelf life; how much retained stock is required? CEO: which city and founder network are real; why will strangers pay instead of buy established brands; which evidence would make us stop; can the team dispatch within10h founder capacity; will we protect contingency rather than chase sunk costs?

### Recorded specialist challenge and decision

Finance challenged single-pack advertising and unbudgeted partner commissions. Accepted: lead cold acquisition with the three-pack, use a fixed INR 40 referral rather than an assumed 10% commission, prohibit referral double-payout, and include gifted-stock cost in channel CAC. The fixed reward costs more than 10% on a single pack but less on a three-pack; single referral contribution is only INR 30.75 before allocated gifts under current assumptions. Do not combine it with a further hidden discount.

Supplier challenged the INR 85 landed assumption and a fixed launch date. Accepted: INR 75–95/200 g is only an indicative planning range pending RFQs; agree **500 accepted finished sealed packs**, including how process loss and factory retention affect output, rather than buying an assumed 100 kg of bulk material. At INR 95 instead of INR 85, three-pack contribution drops by INR 30/order before acquisition. Use an existing recipe only with applicable validated packaging/shelf-life evidence, and postpone launch when evidence is incomplete. Expert retention requirements override discretionary gifting. Neither specialist has supplied an actual supplier quotation.
