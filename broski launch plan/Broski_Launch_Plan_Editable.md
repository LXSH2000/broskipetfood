## 08 / Safe product release

BROSKI / QA AND SHELF LIFE

08 / Safe product release

Do not formulate by copying internet recipes. Use a qualified pet-food technologist and a veterinarian with relevant nutrition expertise to assess the manufacturer’s formulation and intended use. Budget ₹22,000 for the agreed review/testing scope; secure quotes before assuming that scope fits.

Gate | Required evidence

Recipe and process | Signed formulation revision, ingredient traceability, allergen controls, relevant heat/process validation and change-control records.

Testing protocol | Qualified lab specifies microbiological, chemical, moisture/water-activity and ingredient-dependent contaminants; methods and acceptance limits agreed in writing.

Shelf-life evidence | Exact recipe + exact final pouch + storage conditions. Existing validated evidence can support review; a new formulation may need longer studies.

Factory batch | Lot COA, production date, approved coding, count/weight checks, sealed packs and manufacturing records.

Receipt and release | No pests, mould, rancid smell, wrong labels, broken seals or untraceable lots. Quarantine failures; retain agreed samples.

TÜV SÜD India describes pet-food/animal-feed shelf-life work [S9]; it is a laboratory lead, not a booked service or price quote. Verify the actual lab’s accreditation scope for the commissioned methods. Specialists define acceptance thresholds; this plan does not invent universal limits.

Complaints and withdrawal

For suspected contamination, illness, foreign matter or a serious seal failure: stop selling the affected lot, preserve evidence, contact the manufacturer/specialist, trace buyers and record advice/actions. Withdraw or recall as directed. Do not diagnose an animal or dismiss a safety complaint as a refund issue.

Decision gate: Do not print a 6- or 12-month shelf life by borrowing a competitor date. Failed evidence moves the launch date; it does not reduce the test scope.

## 10 / Label and compliance checklist

BROSKI / CONFIRM BEFORE PRINTING

10 / Label and compliance checklist

The Food Safety and Standards Act defines food for human consumption and excludes animal feed [S10]. A human-food FSSAI licence is therefore not a default answer for dog treats. Confirm the actual pet-treat business, plant and local requirements with competent professionals/authorities.

Workstream | Action before launch

State/local operation | Identify city, storage/packing roles and supplier premises; confirm applicable trade, establishment, manufacturing/animal-feed and other permissions.

BIS | IS 11968:2019 is a pet-food standard [S11]. Confirm scope for complementary biscuits and whether any applicable mandatory order/certification exists; do not self-claim certified status.

Legal Metrology | Reviewer confirms manufacturer/packer responsibility and registration, net quantity, MRP inclusive of taxes, identity/address, consumer care, dates and applicable unit-price/ecommerce declarations [S12].

GST | CA confirms current HSN, rate, registration, interstate/ecommerce position, input credit and shipping invoicing. This model’s 18% remains provisional: the historical CBIC FAQ was found, but current amended notifications were not fully reconciled [S18–S19].

Plastic packaging | Map Brand Owner vs Producer/Importer status. CPCB FAQ discusses micro/small Brand Owner EPR relief, not a universal exemption for every MSME role [S13].

Brand/IP | Search and clear name before artwork. Eligible trademark e-filing government fee published at ₹4,500 per class/application [S14]; professional/search fees extra. Udyam registration is free [S3].

Back label: approved ingredients/allergens; substantiated analytical/use/feeding/storage information; manufacturer/packer and marketer details; consumer care; traceable lot; manufacture/packing and justified best-before information; approved MRP and applicable declarations. Front: accurate name, dog-treat use, net 200 g, brand. A final legal label review is required.

Decision gate: Do not print therapeutic, complete-and-balanced, hypoallergenic, dental or “vet approved” claims, certification marks or generated endorsements without substantiation and permission.
